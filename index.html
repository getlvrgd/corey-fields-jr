Sound familiar

New construction is easy to start.
It is hard to start well.

Most investors who lose money on a first build are not reckless. They have made money in real estate before. They treat ground-up like a bigger flip, and by the time they see what was coming, the capital is already in the ground.

01

New construction is not a rehab with a longer timeline

Different lender, different risks, different order of operations. The flip playbook breaks in the first month.

02

Soft costs land before the loan funds

Survey, site plan, architect, permits, interest reserves. They come out of your pocket first. Leave one blank and the budget is wrong on day one.

03

The lot looks cheap until it is not

Grading, utilities, drainage, historic district rules. I've filmed a site prep budget go from $15K to $30K on my own build.

04

The GC you hire becomes your risk

Vetting a builder is an investor skill. Pick on price alone and you find out what the bid left out mid-build.

05

The exit gets picked too late

Build-to-sell or build-to-rent changes the product, the loan, and the numbers. It is a day-one decision, not a move-in-day one.

06

Everyone you can call is selling something

The lot, the loan, the build. What you actually need is someone who will look at your numbers and tell you stop, or go.

The coaching

Six phases of one-on-one coaching

My coaching runs on the six-phase build path my father, Corey Fields Sr., and I teach. You buy the lot, hire the GC, and deploy the capital. I work the decisions with you at every phase, so you make them with an operator's eyes before your money is committed.

This is not GC training, and I am not building it for you. Here is what each phase covers and what you walk away with.

  1. Phase 01

    Team assembly

    Before a lot is under contract, get clear on what you are building toward and who is on your side of the table.

    • Your goal, written down. Build-to-sell, build-to-rent, or both, worked backwards from your capital.
    • Who you need before you need them. Lender, surveyor, civil engineer, architect, attorney.
    • An honest readiness check, including whether the right move is to wait.

    You finish withA clear exit strategy and the short list of people your first build needs.

  2. Phase 02

    Lot readiness

    Cheap land gets expensive in the site work. Price the lot the way a builder would before you make an offer.

    • Zoning and what the lot actually lets you build.
    • Survey, topography, and utilities, and what it costs to bring them to the house.
    • Site prep and grading, the line I watched double on my own build.
    • Historic district and permitting rules that change the timeline.

    You finish withA go or no-go on the lot, with the site costs written in before you offer.

  3. Phase 03

    Bankable plan

    A plan a lender will fund, on a budget with no blanks in it.

    • The four cost buckets. Land, soft costs, hard costs, contingency.
    • Soft costs that come before the construction loan funds, priced up front.
    • Carrying costs and interest reserves for the real timeline, not the hopeful one.
    • Build-to-sell and build-to-rent numbers, side by side.

    You finish withA Deal Viability Worksheet with every line filled in.

  4. Phase 04

    GC relationship

    You hire the builder. You do not have to become one. You do need to know what good looks like.

    • What to ask in a GC interview, and what the answers tell you.
    • Reading a bid for what it leaves out.
    • References and past work, checked instead of assumed.
    • Draw schedule and contract terms, talked through before you sign.

    You finish withA GC you chose on more than price, and a bid you understand line by line.

  5. Phase 05

    Build execution

    You and your GC run the build. I am there for the decisions that come up once the dirt moves.

    • Deal review calls as decisions come up. Change orders, delays, surprises.
    • What to watch at each stage. Foundation, framing, final grading and drainage.
    • Budget against actuals, so contingency gets spent on purpose.

    You finish withDecisions made with an operator's read, while they can still change the outcome.

  6. Phase 06

    Finish and exit

    The exit gets planned in phase one and executed here.

    • Sell or hold, checked against the numbers you started with.
    • What went to plan and what did not, turned into a checklist for build two.

    You finish withA clear exit decision and a process you can repeat on the next build.

FAQ

01How is this different from the $27 Before You Build guide?

The guide is a 15-page read on what every investor should understand before a first residential new build. It is a strong first step. Coaching is me working your actual lot, budget, and GC decisions with you.

02How is this different from the $997 New Build Orientation Program?

The Orientation Program is self-paced. You work through the modules on your own time. Coaching is direct. Same frameworks, different seat. You get my read on your deal, not just the curriculum.

03Is this GC training?

No. You will not learn takeoffs, sub management, or how to swing a hammer. This is the investor seat. What to buy, who to hire, what it really costs, and when to walk.

04Will you build my project for me?

No. You own the deal, the capital, and the decisions. You hire your own GC. I advise.

05Do you guarantee returns?

No. New construction can lose money, and anyone promising margins is selling something. What I promise is process. I work your pre-construction checklist with you until it is decision-ready, and I show up.

06I do not invest in Atlanta. Can I still apply?

I build in Atlanta. If your market is somewhere else, tell me on your application. We decide on fit before anyone commits to anything.

07What does it cost?

I go over pricing on the call, once I know your deal and we both know it is a fit. Nothing gets committed before that.
